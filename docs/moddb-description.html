AstraTerra replaces Vintage Story's night sky with 5,044 catalog stars, 50 deep-sky objects, five planets, nine annual meteor showers, and four returning comets. The sky changes with the hour, season, latitude, and longitude. For Vintage Story 1.22+.

Mark a year of sunrises on a Sky Disc. Measure lights with a Sextant and write the readings into a book. Draw constellations through a telescope. Cut an astrolabe plate for your latitude and use your own records to plan another observation.

Alpha

Some models, recipes, and bits of UI are placeholders. Bug reports and screenshots belong on the issue tracker.

Recent releases

  • Somebody else's disc (v0.10). Pan bony soil and a finished bronze Sky Disc may come up: a year of another observer's sunrises and one constellation, scribed at a latitude that is not yours. It refuses your marks until you carry it home.
  • Longitude (v0.9). World X shifts the sun, daylight, and star field. Noon is local instead of happening everywhere at once.
  • Clock display. The server keeps universal time. The character panel and instruments can show local solar time, universal time, or whole-hour zones.
  • Sun and stars. The star field stays aligned with the visible sun on worlds with custom year lengths.
  • Moon art. Choose AstraTerra pixel art, photographic textures, or Vintage Story's original moon.

Server owners can set LongitudeAwareSun to false in ModConfig/astraterra.json to keep Vintage Story's single time zone.

The sky

Apparent magnitude controls each star's brightness and size. Fifty Stellarium deep-sky plates stay faint until a telescope resolves them. The Milky Way sits behind the catalog.

Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn move on Keplerian orbits. Nine meteor showers return during fixed parts of the world year. Four comets follow their own schedules and point their tails away from the sun. Miss Halley's first pass and the next one is in world year 78. Plan accordingly.

Instruments

▾  Sky Disc

Form one from clay and fire it, or craft copper or bronze. At sunrise or sunset, sneak and hold right click under open sky to scratch the sun's place on the horizon. Keep marking until the band reaches an edge and turns back. The disc then gives year length, latitude, east or west, and the next solstice.

Right click reads it. Scroll turns it. Sneak-right-click the ground to set it down, or stand five in a mold rack. Hold it up at night and left-drag between stars to engrave one connected constellation. A fired clay disc will not take a new figure.

Nothing is cut into a disc without loose flint or a knife somewhere in the hotbar — marks and figures alike.

▾  Telescope and constellation book

Hold right click to use the scope. Scroll changes zoom. Middle click cycles Observe, Create Constellation, Inspect Constellation, and Remove Segment. Drawing needs a writable book in the left hand plus ink and quill. The lines and names are stored in the book.

Under magnification, planets become discs. Saturn has rings. Jupiter's four Galilean moons and five of Saturn's moons move independently and disappear when their planet hides them.

▾  Sextant

Hold right click and center the sight on the sun, moon, a star, a planet, or a comet. The readout gives altitude above the horizon. Middle click cycles the equatorial and horizontal grids.

Sneak while sighting to write an observation into the book in your left hand. Ink and quill are required. The entry records angle, bearing, brightness, position, and time, but not what you aimed at. Use .stars sightings to group observations and .stars classify <set> star|wanderer|comet [name] to record your conclusion.

Sighting the sun currently costs you nothing. A real navigator would use a shade glass.

▾  Calibrated Astrolabe

Recover a ruined astrolabe and fit it with a brass plate. After dusk, sneak-hold right click under open sky to cut the plate for that latitude. Travel far enough and it asks to be recut.

Hold right click with a written book in the left hand to plan an observation. Middle click changes target. Scroll moves the forecast by an hour; sneak-scroll moves it by seven days. The readout gives direction, altitude, rising or setting, and next transit. It also says whether the answer came from your sightings, a recorded figure, or the comet almanac.

Other controls

  • Lie down: press Z. Move, jump, or press Z again to stand. The binding is remappable, and the instruments still work from the ground.
  • .stars calendar full|clock|none controls how much of Vintage Story's date and hour appears. Hide the date if the Sky Disc should earn its keep.
  • .stars solar-system pixel|photo changes the planet textures.
  • .stars moon pixel|photo|vanilla changes the moon.
  • .stars starfield astraterra|both|vanilla switches between the catalog sky and Vintage Story's cubemap.

Other worlds

Another mod can replace any of AstraTerra's catalogs — the stars, the planets, the comets, the meteor showers — and add near bodies, which are the parent planet and sibling moons of a world that is itself a moon. They are shaded from the real sun direction and are valid Sextant targets. AstraExtera uses it.

How we test

Every change goes through tests for the astronomy math, catalogs, instruments, and saved data. CI builds against Vintage Story 1.22.2, packages the same ZIP players install, and builds the docs. Before a release, we deploy that ZIP and work through the in-game checklist after a full restart. The tests catch logic errors. They cannot tell us whether a telescope, moon, animation, or item model actually looks right.

That is why I also test each release manually on a 24 GB M3 MacBook Air with a mostly vanilla-plus set of mods I regularly use: Carry On, Butchering, Better Ruins, Blood Trail, QP Chisel, Buzzwords, Terra Pretty, Watershed, D.E.A.D., Farseer, Footsteps, Food Shelves, Hydrate or Diedrate, RealSmoke, Place On Slabs, Terrain Slabs, Tankards, Vanilla More Molds, Waterfall, and others.

I wish Vintage Story offered an easier way to automate this kind of compatibility testing. If the developers provide one, I intend to add it to this process.
